## Unified methodology after 18 source-reviewed lessons

This synthesis combines Phase 1 Lessons 1–14 with Phase 2 Lessons 1–4. It is the current unified form of “استراتيجية الذكاء,” not a declaration that Phase 2 is finished; four Phase 2 lessons remain. The lesson audits below remain the source ledger whenever this synthesis needs correction.

### 1. Scope and governing principle

- The methodology is gold-only for the future `XAUUSD.m` bot.
- No named tool is a standalone signal. Support/resistance, trendlines, channels, FVG, BPR, OB, Fibonacci, Pivot, candle patterns, and reversal/continuation models supply different parts of one decision chain.
- A partial setup is skipped. Price moving correctly afterward does not retroactively validate an entry that lacked confirmation.
- All displayed prices, point distances, account sizes, returns, lot sizes, R multiples, candle counts, waiting times, and personal anecdotes are educational examples unless later broker-specific testing defines them.

### 2. Unified decision chain

`higher-frame map → broad trend and closed-D1 bias → market regime → governing structure and liquidity → value location → POI stack → lower-frame activation → retest/repricing → entry → structural invalidation and targets → management`

Each derived zone must retain its source timeframe. Higher-frame context is not erased when execution is refined on a lower frame.

### 3. Top-down preparation

1. Build the map from completed Monthly, Weekly, Daily, then H4 structure. Preserve meaningful highs, lows, closes, old pivots, and major zones.
2. Independently derive the broad trend, next-day Daily bias, market regime, and current setup direction from the latest eligible closed OHLC and validated structure. None is a fixed user-supplied setting. Daily bias is assessed only after the relevant D1 candle closes.
3. Use H4/H1 to define the active governing wave and major POIs, M15 for the intraday map and nested zones, and M1/M3/M5 for activation and execution.
4. Treat indecisive Daily context as caution: reduce conviction or skip consequential trades rather than inventing a direction.

### 4. Direction and market regime

- First classify the market as trending, correcting, or ranging.
- Broad trend, closed-D1 bias, market regime, and current setup direction are separate dynamic analysis outputs. The future bot must recalculate them autonomously from market data and the reviewed rules on every relevant analysis cycle; it must not wait for a user, screenshot, or manually fixed direction.
- These states change at different speeds and on different evidence. Broad trend changes through validated higher-frame structure; Daily bias updates from the applicable closed D1 context; market regime changes as trend/correction/range structure develops; setup direction is selected only after the current POI and confirmation chain are evaluated.
- A broad trend and a Daily corrective bias may point in opposite directions. A corrective trade against the broad trend is allowed only when supported by the closed-D1 bias, a higher-frame POI, structural change, and the full lower-frame confirmation chain.
- Daily bias is a preferred-direction prior, not a permanent lock. A small opposing pattern is insufficient, but a qualified break/retest with stronger or repeated confirmation can cause the current setup to be skipped, reclassified, or—when the source rules support it—taken in the newly confirmed direction. Re-run the complete analysis instead of forcing the old bias.
- In a range, use a Parallel Channel through meaningful boundaries. Do not enter from the middle. Prefer resolution in the working bias direction; a break against bias needs materially stronger confirmation or is skipped.

### 5. Structural highs/lows and liquidity

- Most M5/M15 swings are internal structure and liquidity, not major reversal anchors.
- Major governing highs/lows are anchored primarily from H4, Daily, Weekly, and Monthly.
- In bearish structure, a true high is validated when the bearish impulse from it breaks the governing low. A later rise remains corrective until its following bearish leg breaks that low. Mirror the rule in bullish structure: a true low must drive the break of the governing high.
- Liquidity pools are context and targets, never entries by themselves. Wait for a sweep and the subsequent structural evidence.
- The structurally relevant break is the high that established the low, or the low that established the high—not the nearest arbitrary micro-pivot.

### 6. Support/resistance, trendlines, and channels

- Support/resistance are behavioral zones, drawn as areas rather than traded as exact first-touch lines.
- Resistance becomes support only after break/close, return, and successful reclaim/rejection; support becomes resistance through the mirrored sequence.
- Trendlines use meaningful body/close-based anchors. A line break without displacement/FVG and follow-through is insufficient.
- Channels map wave geometry and range boundaries. A valid breakout workflow is boundary break with displacement/FVG outside the channel, retest/repricing, then lower-frame confirmation. Edge trades inside a range still require rejection/activation.

### 7. FVG and BPR selection

- A standard FVG is a three-candle wick-to-wick inefficiency. It is a location/repricing zone, not a signal.
- Rank FVGs by direction, active range, premium/discount location, freshness, higher-frame context, and nested lower-frame structure.
- Opposing overlapping imbalances can form a BPR used as a reconciled repricing area.
- When several adjacent FVGs are created by the same confirmed displacement, they may be grouped into one composite zone and their combined midpoint used as a contextual balance reference. This does not make every nearby gap cluster tradable.
- A failed FVG/zone must change state when structure invalidates it; do not preserve a permanently bullish or bearish label after failure.

### 8. Order Block qualification and state

A qualified OB requires the combined evidence:

1. prior liquidity sweep;
2. displacement through the structurally relevant governing point;
3. FVG/imbalance creation.

Mark the defining candle as a full high-to-low zone unless a later source-tested refinement applies. Distinguish Fresh, mitigated/noisy, failed, and Breaker states. A reaction alone does not prove the block will hold; a failed OB can reverse role as a Breaker. Lower-frame blocks are stronger when nested inside a higher-frame FVG/POI.

### 9. Fibonacci and value location

- Measure the entire active validated impulse from its governing low to high, or the bearish mirror. Recalculate when a new structural impulse is confirmed; do not treat Fibonacci levels as permanent chart lines.
- The 50% midpoint grades value and risk. For a bullish impulse, buying above 50% is premium/expensive and below it is cleaner discount; mirror this for sells.
- Earlier FVG-entry examples reject expensive directional FVGs above/below the applicable 50% gate. The later clarification makes 50% a location/risk grade rather than a universal absolute ban: an expensive trade is higher risk and needs stronger justification, while 50% itself neither triggers entry nor guarantees a retracement.
- The 61.8% area is meaningful only as confluence with structure, FVG/OB, support, and lower-frame behavior.

### 10. Patterns and candles

- Double/Triple Tops or Bottoms, Head-and-Shoulders variants, wedges, flags, triangles, hammers, rejection candles, and engulfing candles are confirmation tools at a qualified POI.
- A recognizable shape is not enough. A reversal model must complete and activate through its valid neckline/boundary break.
- Prefer a break with displacement/FVG, followed by repricing/retest and a confirming close. A candle name without this context is ignored.
- Continuation models use their own structural invalidation and measured projection; breaking the model’s governing base invalidates its target.

### 11. Default entry state machine

1. Direction and active range are defined from higher frames.
2. Price reaches a qualified higher-frame POI in acceptable value.
3. On M1/M3/M5, price sweeps local liquidity or forms a valid reversal/continuation structure.
4. The relevant neckline, channel, trendline, or governing micro-structure breaks with displacement and FVG.
5. Price returns to reprice the execution FVG, BPR, refined OB, or reclaimed support/resistance zone.
6. Rejection/engulfing and the required close confirm that the area held.
7. Enter with protection beyond the newly confirmed structural invalidation, allowing for the actual `XAUUSD.m` spread/specification.

If any required state is absent, skip the entry even if price later reaches the expected target.

### 12. Range-specific execution

- Do not trade from the channel midpoint.
- Primary path: break in the working bias direction → displacement/FVG outside the range → retest → M3/M1 activation → entry.
- Several adjacent breakout FVGs may be treated as a composite zone.
- A M3 Double Bottom/Top requires a valid neckline break and FVG, then repricing. M1 confirmation is optional and noisier when the higher execution structure is already sufficient.
- A decisive range break is not countertraded merely because price looks extended; wait for its retest or for a separately qualified reversal.

### 13. Direct-touch exception

The default remains confirmation before entry. The reviewed narrow exception is a fully qualified M5 OB that swept liquidity, broke the correct structure, created FVG, and was nested inside/supportively aligned with an M15 FVG. Its first-touch entry was accepted; the later Double Bottom was additional, not mandatory.

Do not generalize this to ordinary OBs. On return to a normal full-candle OB, wait on M1/M3 for an activated reversal model before tightening protection. Exact eligibility for an automated direct-touch exception is still undefined.

### 14. Pivots

- Daily and Weekly Pivot use `(High + Low + Close) / 3` from the selected completed candle on the corresponding timeframe.
- Pivot is a control/support-resistance reference, not a touch trigger.
- On M5, a close/reclaim plus retest can support the direction; close below plus retest can target the lower reference. A first apparently valid reclaim may stop before a later setup succeeds.
- Broker-time day/week boundaries, eligible rollover candle, rounding, close tolerance, retest tolerance, and target selection remain undefined.

### 15. Stops, targets, additions, and exits

- Stop-loss is a normal controlled cost and belongs beyond logical structural invalidation, not at an arbitrary desired distance.
- Compress a wide higher-frame stop only after new confirmed lower-frame invalidation structure forms. The verbal H1-OB guidance allowing roughly 30–50% reduction and rejecting 95% compression is qualitative; its baseline and anchor are not an executable formula.
- Targets may use nearby structural liquidity, the opposite range edge, pattern/measured-move projection, or sequential Fibonacci/Pivot references suited to the execution timeframe.
- After a confirmed close through one Fibonacci level, the next may become the objective. If momentum stops and price becomes sideways/choppy, an early exit is allowed rather than forcing the final projection.
- Do not move stops or targets from anxiety. Protect only at a structurally defensible level.
- Add only when the original trade is protected and moving correctly, and the add-on has its own complete same-direction setup and stop. Never average into a losing position.

### 16. Unified no-trade conditions

Skip when any of the following applies:

- incomplete confirmation chain;
- entry from the middle of a range;
- support/resistance, liquidity, FVG, OB, Fibonacci, Pivot, pattern, or candle used alone;
- ordinary OB first touch without activation;
- unqualified trendline/channel or neckline break;
- choppy overlap presented as displacement;
- counter-bias pattern without the stronger/repeated confirmation and valid structural or range resolution required to overturn the prior analysis;
- noisy/mitigated POI without sufficient refinement;
- expensive directional FVG in the reviewed FVG-entry workflow;
- stop placement that has no structural invalidation;
- missing trade is preferable to inventing retrospective validity.

### 17. Not yet automatable

The combined methodology still lacks tested numeric definitions for: swing detection; meaningful pivots; liquidity pools and sweep tolerance; body-versus-wick break rules; displacement strength; FVG minimum size and grouping; zone merging; freshness/mitigation; trendline/channel anchors; pattern ratios and activation; premium/discount range selection; direct-touch eligibility; close/retest tolerances; spread buffers; sideways/chop detection; stop-compression anchors; target selection; broker session boundaries; and cross-provider wick discrepancies.

Future implementation must autonomously derive and continuously re-evaluate broad trend, closed-D1 bias, market regime, active structure, and current setup direction from the live MT5 history and specifications of `XAUUSD.m`. It must not depend on user-supplied chart images or a manually fixed direction, and must not silently infer a sweep visible only on another provider. No trading code is authorized until the user explicitly says “نفذ”.

## Lesson 4 audit — primary and secondary price channels (source verified)

The fourth lesson was reviewed from its transcript and gold-chart frames. A price channel is not simply a trendline with a parallel line added. A valid descending primary channel needs two highs with a low between them; a valid ascending primary channel needs two lows with a high between them. The channel is only considered formed after those pivots are present, and it represents sloped support/resistance along the wave.

**Confirmed channel context:** A smaller secondary channel can form inside a primary channel, especially during an internal sideways movement. It remains a structure inside the larger wave, not a standalone entry instruction. A break can shift the market from an ascending to a descending channel, requiring a newly drawn channel for the new direction.

**Confirmed continuation projection:** After a strong channel break, the instructor copies the channel geometry to project the next parallel path, aligning the prior relevant boundary with the new support/resistance. In the illustrated progression, a prior boundary becomes the new channel’s middle line. He limits such successive copies to roughly two or three, rather than extending them indefinitely.

**Confirmation and targets:** Repeated channel respect followed by a break on a later touch (the third or fourth was cited) increases the breakout confirmation; the example waits for a retest and shows a price gap/FVG around that retest. The next projected channel boundary or swing high/low is used as the contextual objective.

**Why:** The lesson treats channels as a map of a complete wave and its likely sloped reaction areas, while repeatedly rejecting entry before confirmation.

**How to apply:** Do not automate a channel trade yet. “Strong break,” valid pivot selection, touch counting, channel-copy alignment, retest tolerance, and the FVG condition remain non-numeric. Treat the segment about the instructor’s live trade, the 50% area, head-and-shoulders, and news as chart-specific commentary until those subjects are independently taught and source-verified.

## Lesson 5 audit — fair value gaps and associated zones (source verified)

The fifth lesson was reviewed from its transcript and gold-chart frames. A Fair Value Gap (FVG) is drawn across three candles using the wicks of the first and third candles. A bullish FVG is the untraded zone between the first candle’s high and the third candle’s low after upward displacement. A bearish FVG is the untraded zone between the third candle’s high and the first candle’s low after downward displacement.

**Confirmed use constraint:** An FVG is not a standalone trade signal. The instructor requires it to agree with an area of interest, Fibonacci, market structure, or other context. His “fuel” description is explanatory; it is not a deterministic claim that price must fill every FVG and reverse.

**Inversion FVG:** In the illustrated bearish-FVG example, price fully closed through the FVG to the upside; it was then treated as an inversion FVG, changing from resistance into support on the return. The lesson’s direct illustration establishes that exact case; do not assume every partial fill or all mirror-image cases qualify until a measurable rule is established.

**Balanced Price Range (BPR):** The visual example shows an opposing bullish FVG closing against a bearish FVG, leaving their common/reconciled area as the BPR. It can then be revisited as a repricing area before continuation. The lesson does not yet define a minimum overlap or an objective BPR boundary.

**Confirmed breakout context:** The instructor again requires a visible FVG with a trendline or channel break, and shows a retest of the broken trend/FVG before the move turns from bearish to bullish.

**Gold trade example, not a reusable rule:** The live example framed the broader daily context as bearish, then treated a bullish H4 FVG reaction, engulfing candle, H1 structure shift, trend/channel break, and BPR repricing as grounds for a smaller corrective buy toward a 50% target. **Why:** The instructor explicitly warns against trading every visible gap and illustrates the same FVG differently according to structure and confluence.

**How to apply:** A future gold-only strategy may use properly measured FVGs as filters or retest zones, never as sole entries. Wait for source-defined rules for POIs, Fibonacci, order blocks, structure shifts, engulfing candles, stop placement, and target selection before encoding the example.

## Lesson 8 audit — continuation patterns and projection targets (source verified)

The eighth lesson was reviewed from its transcript and gold-chart frames. The two preferred continuation patterns are the parallel Flag and the converging Pennant. They may confirm or add to an existing directional thesis; they are not substitutes for the initial higher-timeframe setup.

**Validity conditions:** A strong displacement/impulse must precede the pattern. The pullback should normally remain within 38.2%; up to 50% may be accepted, especially on a larger timeframe, but a deeper correction invalidates the continuation pattern. For this pattern boundary the source explicitly draws the trendline on candle wicks, unlike its body/Line-Chart method for structural trendlines.

**Confirmed entry sequence:** Wait for a boundary break with FVG/displacement, then wait for a retest rather than entering immediately. Refine the retest on a lower timeframe with a reversal confirmation such as Double Bottom/Top or Head-and-Shoulders, including its neckline break. The source generally recommends M15 or higher for the pattern itself; M5 can serve as refinement, while still lower charts are described as excessively manipulated.

**Price-range projection:** Measure the preceding impulse and clone that range from the breakout to estimate continuation. The instructor prefers liquidity-to-liquidity/body-to-body measurement over wick-to-wick, while acknowledging the latter as the common classical method. This is a source preference, not yet a universal algorithm.

**Stops and setup lifetime:** The examples place the stop beyond the pattern’s relevant extreme, with a small spread allowance where appropriate. If a setup already reaches its projected target, it is consumed and should not be traded later merely because price returns to the old breakout/retest area.

**Extension markers:** The displayed Fibonacci projection levels are 1.000, 1.382, 1.618, and 1.840. They are candidate target markers. The spoken example divides position size across several exits, but it does not map every partial close unambiguously to one level; the lot numbers are illustrative and must not become bot sizing rules.

**Excluded continuation shapes:** The source deliberately does not adopt wedges or rectangles as preferred continuation setups because their apparent breakouts can reverse and create ICT/SMC manipulation ambiguity. This is a methodology choice, not a claim that those shapes can never work.

**Conflict filter:** If price reaches a planned buy area but forms a valid bearish continuation pattern, do not take the buy merely because the Fibonacci area was reached; wait for clarity.

**Why:** The instructor treats continuation as a shallow pause within established displacement and requires breakout quality, retest, and lower-timeframe confirmation before projecting the prior impulse.

**How to apply:** A future XAUUSD.m implementation would need objective impulse strength, pattern geometry, 38.2%/50% tolerance, FVG and retest rules, timeframe mapping, projection anchoring, stop buffer, and exit allocation before automation. Do not derive lot sizing from the lesson’s examples.

## Lesson 9 audit — real structure pivots and swing liquidity (source verified)

The ninth lesson is preparatory vocabulary for market structure and Order Blocks, not a standalone entry model. Its chart frames and transcript distinguish a **real/structural high or low** from an ordinary intra-wave high or low.

**Structural pivots:** Build the larger structure from the terminal high or low of the principal wave — the anchor that begins the resulting displacement — not from every fluctuation inside that wave. The instructor illustrates waves as commonly having five components, but does not provide an Elliott-wave counting method or a machine-testable wave rule.

The source calls these locations liquidity concentrations. A pivot that is ordinary on one timeframe can be a swing on a lower timeframe; status is timeframe-relative.

**Liquidity qualification:** For the advanced methodology, the instructor says a high/low is not treated as a real structural pivot until it has swept the liquidity before it. The examples show that taking a swing pool can produce a reversal, including a small reversal before continuation; this is context, not a trade trigger.

**Equal extrema:** Equal highs/lows are shown as separate liquidity configurations within a larger move. The lesson does not state a numerical equality tolerance, an automated sweep definition, or whether any wick versus close test validates them.

**Structure change:** Breaking a real structural pivot signals a possible change of market structure rather than merely a correction. The exact confirmation requirements (wick or close, timeframe hierarchy, distance, retest, and distinction between a liquidity sweep and a true break) are deliberately left for the later structure lessons.

**Why:** The instructor wants Order Block and liquidity analysis anchored to meaningful wave endpoints, avoiding false structural signals from internal fluctuations.

**How to apply:** A future XAUUSD.m implementation must keep separate: local three-point swings, timeframe-selected structural pivots, liquidity sweeps, and confirmed structure breaks. Do not automate them from this lesson alone.
